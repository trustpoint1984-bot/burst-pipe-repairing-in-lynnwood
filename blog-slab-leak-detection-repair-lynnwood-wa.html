What Exactly Is a Concrete Slab Leak?

In modern residential architecture across Lynnwood and South Snohomish County, many single-story and split-level homes were constructed on "slab-on-grade" concrete foundations. During original construction, hot and cold copper water supply lines were laid directly onto gravel subgrade and encased beneath poured concrete.

Over decades, four primary factors cause these encased pipes to fail:

  • Thermal Expansion Friction: As hot water flows, copper tubing expands and contracts, continually rubbing against sharp gravel and coarse concrete aggregate until a friction hole forms.
  • Electrolytic Soil Corrosion: Chemical reactions between damp Snohomish County clay soils and copper produce galvanic pitting that perforates pipe walls.
  • Kinked Lines During Construction: Tubing bent carelessly by original builders creates localized turbulence zones that erode metal from the inside out.
  • Water Hammer Shock: High street pressure slamming into closed solenoid valves in modern washing machines vibrates underground lines until joints rupture.

5 Classic Symptoms of an Under-Slab Leak in Lynnwood Homes

1. Hot Spots on Linoleum or Tile Floors

Walking barefoot across your kitchen or hallway and noticing a distinct warm square or damp patch indicates a leaking hot water line beneath.

2. Water Meter Spinning When Taps Are Off

Turn off every plumbing fixture in your home and inspect your Alderwood Water District meter. If the red low-flow triangle or digital flow rate is moving, water is leaking.

3. The Sound of Rushing Water in Quiet Rooms

Hearing faint running water or an electrical-like hissing noise coming from beneath floorboards even when all toilets and faucets are completely idle.

4. Sudden Spikes in Water or Gas/Electric Bills

A ruptured hot water line forces your water heater to run 24 hours a day, causing sudden $200-$500 increases in monthly utility expenses.

How We Pinpoint Under-Slab Leaks Without Jackhammers

Old-school plumbing companies used sledgehammers to guess where a leak might be, destroying thousands of dollars in hardwood flooring. Our Lynnwood technicians utilize cutting-edge acoustic science:

  • Acoustic Ultrasonic Ground Microphones: Pressurized water escaping a pipe creates high-frequency sound waves. Our sensors amplify frequencies between 500 Hz and 2,500 Hz, filtering out room noise to isolate the leak epicenter within 6 inches.
  • FLIR Thermal Infrared Scanning: We trace high-resolution thermal gradients radiating through flooring materials to visually confirm underground hot water pooling.
  • Non-Toxic Tracer Gas Detection: For complex or silent micro-leaks, we inject food-grade nitrogen/hydrogen tracer gas into isolated water lines and use helium-calibrated sniffers to detect gas escaping through concrete pores.

Repair Options: Direct Jackhammering vs. Overhead PEX Reroute

Once the leak is pinpointed, Lynnwood homeowners have two repair paths:

Method Invasiveness Future Risk Recommendation
Direct Slab Penetration (Jackhammer) High: Breaks tile/wood flooring; concrete dust High: Other aged copper lines under slab remain at risk Only for isolated single breaches in unfinished rooms
Overhead PEX-A Reroute (Bypass) Minimal: Clean routing through attic or joists Zero: Abandons old subterranean line permanently Gold Standard: 25-Year Warranty, 100% Floor Safety

In over 85% of cases, we recommend an Overhead PEX-A Reroute. We cap off the corroded under-slab line at both ends and run a brand-new, freeze-resistant expansion line through your attic or upper joists. Your beautiful flooring remains untouched, and you never have to worry about an under-slab leak on that line again.
