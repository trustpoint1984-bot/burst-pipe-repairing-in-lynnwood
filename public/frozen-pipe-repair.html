The Science of Freezing Pipes

Why Frozen Pipes Burst When The Thaw Begins

Water expands by roughly 9% as it freezes into ice inside copper or PEX tubing. Contrary to popular belief, the pipe rarely bursts at the exact spot where the ice forms. Instead, the ice blockage expands like an airtight piston, causing pressure downstream between the ice clog and closed faucets to skyrocket over 2,000 PSI.

When ambient temperatures rise or you attempt to warm the line, the ice melts, allowing thousands of gallons of pressurized water to flood through the split fissure into your ceilings, walls, and crawlspaces.

WARNING: Never use an open flame torch to thaw pipes! Hundreds of house fires in Washington are caused each winter by homeowners using propane torches on pipes near wooden studs and insulation.
