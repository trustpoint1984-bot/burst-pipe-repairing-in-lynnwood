Where to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Lynnwood Homes

Depending on the era your Lynnwood home was built (1960s rambler vs. modern 2010s construction), the main water entrance is typically located in one of four places:

Location 1: The Finished Basement or Crawlspace Entry Wall

In homes with basements or crawlspaces, the 3/4-inch main copper line enters through the concrete foundation facing the street. Look low along the foundation wall for a brass valve located directly above where the pipe pierces the concrete floor or wall.

Location 2: Attached Garage Near the Water Heater

In slab-on-grade homes common in Alderwood Manor and Martha Lake, the main water service line rises inside the garage near the water heater platform or inside an insulated corner drywall bump-out.

Location 3: Utility Closet or Laundry Room

In townhomes and split-level houses, builders often concealed the shutoff valve behind a hinged white plastic access panel in the laundry room or primary utility closet.

Location 4: Street Meter Box at the Property Line

Near the street sidewalk or driveway entrance, locate an oval cast iron or heavy composite meter box lid stamped "WATER". Inside sits the Alderwood Water District meter and an upstream curb stop valve.

Ball Valve vs. Gate Valve: How to Turn Them Off

Modern Style

Quarter-Turn Ball Valve

Features a straight lever handle. When the lever is parallel to the pipe, water is ON. Turn the lever 90 degrees perpendicular to the pipe until it stops. Water is now 100% OFF.

✓ Fast, reliable, virtually impossible to seize.
Older Style

Multi-Turn Wheel Gate Valve

Features a round circular wheel handle (resembling an outdoor garden faucet). Turn the wheel clockwise ("Righty-Tighty") several full rotations until it seats firmly.

⚠️ Caution: Prone to mineral rust seizure. Never force with a wrench.

How to Shut Off the Street Meter Box During an Emergency

If your indoor valve is missing or seized, follow these steps to shut water at the municipal curb stop:

  1. Remove the Meter Lid: Use a flathead screwdriver or claw hammer to pry off the heavy cast iron lid near your front curb. Beware of spiders or dirt.
  2. Locate the Brass Valve: Look on the street side of the water meter for a raised brass valve with an oblong flange top.
  3. Use a 5-Foot Water Meter Key: Place the slotted end of a steel curb key over the valve flange. Turn the valve 90 degrees clockwise until the flange eyelet aligns with the stationary padlock eyelet.
  4. Verify Meter Has Stopped: Ensure the tiny leak indicator star or digital register has stopped moving entirely.
